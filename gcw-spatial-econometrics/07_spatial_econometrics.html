Spatial econometric analysis

Diagnostics, candidate models, and author-led selection

Purpose and scope

This Quarto notebook uses R throughout to audit, compare, and visualise the spatial-analysis sequence for the nine primary relationships. It reads the prepared 2024 model fits and diagnostics in results/validation/; rendering the notebook does not refit the 168 upstream candidate models. To regenerate those estimates, run python code/scripts/run_spatial_emissions_extension.py from the repository root. The distinction between upstream estimation and this R-based review is explicit so that the numerical provenance remains traceable.

The nine relationships are grouped into three sets:

  1. Capacity loss, relative erosion of GCW, and GCW/GDP as outcomes under the baseline covariates.
  2. The same three outcomes with 2024 emissions added as a predictor.
  3. 2024 emissions as the outcome, with capacity loss, relative erosion, or GCW/GDP added as the focal predictor.

Each relationship is examined under two spatial-weights definitions: Queen contiguity and symmetrised five-nearest neighbours. The outputs also contain auxiliary specifications and matched-sample references. Those are retained for diagnostics but are not counted among the nine primary relationships above.

This notebook exposes evidence for the researcher to review. It does not automatically select a model, choose a weight matrix, interpret coefficients, or treat associations as causal.

3. Step 1 - Moran’s I for the outcome and OLS residuals

Moran’s I is calculated here for the transformed outcome (y_log1p) and the OLS residuals (ols_residual). The first diagnostic describes spatial autocorrelation in the outcome; the second checks whether spatial pattern remains in the residuals of the non-spatial baseline. The table reports the statistic, permutation result, sample size, island/component counts, and the stored Holm-adjusted value where available.

Queen contiguity connects units sharing a boundary. Symmetric kNN-5 connects each unit to its five nearest neighbours and symmetrises the links. These define alternative neighbourhood matrices, so compare them as separate specifications rather than treating their statistics as interchangeable.

4. Step 2 - Lagrange-multiplier diagnostics after OLS

The LM diagnostics test specific spatial-dependence alternatives relative to the OLS baseline. LM_lag and LM_error are the conventional lag and error tests; their robust counterparts adjust each test for the competing form of dependence. The joint tests are also retained. The reported p-values and Holm-adjusted values are shown without using a significance threshold to choose a final estimator automatically.
